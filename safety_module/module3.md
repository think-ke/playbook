# 🧩 Module 3: Safe Use of AI Products (Safety in Use)

## 1. Topics & Content

### 1.1 Why Safety in Use Matters
Even when AI products are built with Safety by Design principles (Module 2), risks still exist in daily use:

- **Misinformation & Errors**: AI tools may generate factually wrong answers.  
- **Privacy Breaches**: Users may overshare personal or sensitive data.  
- **Over-reliance**: Trusting AI for critical decisions (health, legal, finance).  
- **Exploitation**: Deepfakes, scams, and targeted harassment.  
- **Digital Inequalities**: Vulnerable groups may face disproportionate risks (women, youth, low digital literacy).  

**Kenya Examples:**  
- AI-driven health chatbots misdiagnosing malaria due to incomplete training data.  
- Farmers using agri-tech AI apps that misinterpret local crop diseases.  
- Women politicians targeted with AI-generated deepfake content during elections.  

---

### 1.2 Principles of Safe AI Use
- **Critical Thinking** – Question AI outputs, verify with trusted human sources.  
  - *Example*: Students should fact-check AI-generated essays with textbooks and curriculum guidelines which they can look up online.  

- **Privacy Awareness** – Share only necessary data; beware of phishing scams.  
  - *Example*: Avoid giving national ID numbers, PINs, or geolocation data to unverified apps.

- **Contextual Judgment** – Use AI as a support tool, not a final authority.  
  - *Example*: Farmers can use AI crop diagnosis as a second opinion, not the only decision-maker.  

- **Safety Reporting & Escalation** – Know how to report unsafe experiences.  
  - *Example*: Report harassment to platform safety teams, then escalate to KE-CIRT/CC or the 1195 GBV helpline.  

- **Digital Wellbeing & Balance** – Manage over-dependence on AI in work, study, and personal life.  
  - *Example*: Students relying on AI for all assignments risk skill erosion and also risk overdepedency on AI Bots.  

- **Inclusivity in Use** – Recognize that risks differ by gender, age, location, and literacy.  
  - *Example*: Women face online harassment at higher rates especially on social media.  

---

### 1.3 Everyday Safe Practices

**Do’s**
- Double-check AI health, finance, or legal advice with qualified professionals.  
- Use apps compliant with Kenya’s *Data Protection Act (2019)*.  
- Enable safety settings (multi-factor authentication, strong passwords, language filters).  
- Use AI tools in secure, trusted platforms.  

**Don’ts**
- Don’t share sensitive data (ID number, PIN,Name,Passport number, Address,health records) with unverified apps.  
- Don’t treat AI advice as professional diagnosis or legal judgment.  
- Don’t forward unverified AI-generated news or deep fakes.
- Don’t ignore signs of online harassment or manipulation.  

---

### 1.4 Kenyan Institutional Context
Users in Kenya should know key pathways for safety escalation:

- **ODPC (Office of the Data Protection Commissioner)** → complaints on privacy breaches.  
- **KE-CIRT/CC (National Computer Incident Response Team)** → reporting cyber threats, scams, AI-driven fraud.  
- **NC4 (National Computer Cybercrime Coordination Committee)** → legal enforcement.  
- **1195 Helpline** → GBV support (including tech-facilitated GBV).  
- **Civil Society Support** → FIDA Kenya, Article 19, and other NGOs supporting online safety.

## 2. Learning Outcomes
By the end of this module, participants will be able to:
- Recognize risks users face when interacting with AI products.  
- Apply safe-use principles to everyday interactions with AI.  
- Demonstrate responsible sharing and privacy-protective behaviors.  
- Identify Kenyan institutions and hotlines for reporting unsafe AI use.  
- Promote safe AI practices within their communities or organizations.

## 3. Activities

### Activity 1: AI Safety Do’s & Don’ts Roleplay
- **Scenario**: A learner receives medical advice from a chatbot that seems unsafe.  
- **Task**: Roleplay both a “safe” user (cross-checks with a doctor) and an “unsafe” user (acts without verification).  
- **Debrief**: Which approach reduced harm?  

---

### Activity 2: Fact-Checking Challenge
- **Setup**: Participants query an AI on a civic issue (e.g., Kenya’s election laws).  
- **Task**: Verify AI output using IEBC or official gov.ke sites.  
- **Goal**: Build habits of verification.  

---

### Activity 3: Survivor-Centered TFGBV Case Study
- **Scenario**: A woman journalist receives AI-generated deepfake harassment.  
- **Task**: Groups map a safety response pathway:  
  - *Immediate*: block/report to platform.  
  - *Institutional*: KE-CIRT/CC, ODPC, 1195 helpline.  
  - *Support*: Civil society/legal aid.  
- **Goal**: Highlight gendered harms and support systems.  

---

### Activity 4: Community Safety Campaign Design
- **Task**: In small groups, design a 1-page “Safe Use Tips” poster for:  
  - Youth in schools.  
  - Farmers using agri-tech apps.  
  - Women in politics.  
- **Goal**: Practice tailoring safety guidance to different Kenyan contexts.
