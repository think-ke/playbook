# Module 1: Introduction to AI Safety

## 1. Topics & Content

### 1.1 What is AI Safety?
**AI Safety** = designing, developing, and deploying AI systems in ways that minimize risks to humans, society, and the environment by making the AI sytems responsible ,ethical and fair.

- **Functional Safety** → preventing accidents and unintended harm (e.g., medical AI misdiagnosis).  
- **Ethical Safety** → ensuring fairness, inclusivity, non-discrimination.  
- **Societal Safety** → addressing long-term systemic risks (e.g., disinformation).  

---

### 1.2 Historical & Kenyan Case Studies of Unsafe AI Deployments

**Kenyan Context**
- **Biased Credit Scoring (Fintech & Mobile Loans)**  
  - Algorithms used by mobile lending apps sometimes flag low-income groups or informal workers as high risk.  
  - This disproportionately excludes women, rural farmers, and youth from accessing fair credit.  

- **Facial Recognition in Public Spaces**  
  - Nairobi has piloted AI-powered surveillance cameras.  
  - **Risks**: privacy violations, false positives in law enforcement, and potential misuse against marginalized groups.  

- **Misinformation During Elections**  
  - Social media platforms amplified AI-driven misinformation campaigns in the 2017 and 2022 Kenyan elections as well as the 2024 & 2025 Maadamano protests.  
  - **Risk**: destabilization of democratic processes and public trust.  

**Global Cases**
- **Amazon Recruitment Tool (2018)** — biased against women in hiring.  
- **COMPAS Risk Assessment (2016)** — racial bias in U.S. criminal justice.  
- **Microsoft Tay Bot (2016)** — manipulated into producing offensive content.  
- **Tesla Autopilot (ongoing)** — accidents due to over-reliance and misidentification.  
---

### 1.3 The Difference Between Safety, Ethics, and Security

- **Safety**: Preventing accidents and unintended harm.  
  - *Example (Kenya)*: An AI health chatbot misdiagnosing malaria due to poor training data.  

- **Ethics**: Ensuring fairness, accountability, transparency.  
  - *Example (Kenya)*: Loan-scoring algorithms unfairly disadvantaging informal sector workers.  

- **Security**: Protecting against intentional attacks.  
  - *Example (Kenya)*: AI-powered deepfakes spreading false political content during campaigns.  

**Analogy:**  
- Safety = seatbelts & airbags  
- Ethics = fairness of who gets a driving license  
- Security = protecting cars from hackers  

---

### 1.4 Key Risks in AI
- **Misuse**: AI-generated misinformation during elections.  
- **Bias**: Credit scoring and insurance algorithms disadvantaging low-income communities.  
- **Accidents**: Errors in diagnostic AI used in Kenyan hospitals.  
- **Adversarial Manipulation**: Altered license plates confusing traffic cameras in Nairobi.  

---

## 2. Learning Outcomes
By the end of this module, learners should be able to:
- Define AI safety and explain its importance.  
- Differentiate between safety, ethics, and security.  
- Analyze real-world case studies of unsafe AI (Kenyan and Global).  
- Identify common categories of AI risks.  

---

## 3. Activities

**Case Study Analysis (Group or Individual)**  
- **Case Chosen**: Mobile Loan App Bias in Kenya.  
- **Steps**:  
  1. Read a summary of how Kenyan mobile lending platforms use AI for credit scoring.  
  2. Identify what went wrong: (biased datasets, lack of transparency, over-penalization of informal workers).  
  3. Classify the issue: Is it a safety, ethics, or security problem?  
  4. Propose at least 2 strategies that could have prevented or mitigated the risk.  

**Discussion Questions:**
- Should fintechs be regulated to ensure fairness in AI-based credit scoring?  
- Is algorithmic bias more of a technical flaw or a reflection of existing inequalities in society?  
- How do we balance innovation (digital loans) with caution (preventing exclusion)?
