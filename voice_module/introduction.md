# 1. Introduction

The rapid development of digital technologies has fundamentally reshaped the way societies communicate, learn, and engage with one another. Voice technologies, in particular, now play a central role in education, accessibility, customer service, and civic participation. However, the benefits of these innovations remain unevenly distributed. While major world languages enjoy comprehensive digital representation, many African languages remain excluded from speech technologies.

This exclusion is not merely technical; it carries cultural and economic consequences. When communities cannot use their mother tongues in digital spaces, they risk marginalisation, reduced access to opportunities, and the erosion of linguistic heritage.

The Tech Innovators Network Voice Playbook was created to address this imbalance. It provides a structured and practical framework for collecting, managing, and sharing voice data in ways that are open, ethical, and sustainable.

By emphasising low-resource languages in Kenya ,such as Kikuyu, Dholuo, Maasai, Kalenjin, and Somali,just to name a few, the Playbook highlights the importance of community-led approaches in:

- Preserving linguistic and cultural heritage.  
- Enabling inclusive digital participation.  
- Reducing structural bias in artificial intelligence.  
- Stimulating local innovation and digital economies.  

This Playbook is designed as a living resource. It is modular, adaptable, and meant to evolve through contributions from practitioners, communities, and researchers.

## 1.1 How to Use this Playbook

This Playbook is not intended to be read as a traditional book from start to finish. Instead, it is organised into standalone modules, each covering a specific dimension of voice data collection and use. Users may approach the Playbook in different ways depending on their roles and objectives.

### For Community Leaders and NGOs
- Begin with the **Preparation & Stakeholder Engagement** and **Governance & Ethics** modules.  
- Use the **Capacity Building** chapter to design workshops and training for local participants.  
- Refer to the **Pilot Projects** section for real-world examples that can be adapted locally.  

### For Developers and Innovators
- Focus on the **Data Collection Framework** and **Open Data Infrastructure** modules.  
- Engage with the **Toolkit** section to integrate open-source components into local projects.  
- Explore the **Interoperability** guidance to connect datasets with existing global repositories.  

### For Researchers and Academics
- Refer to the **Impact Metrics** module for frameworks on dataset evaluation.  
- Examine the **Governance & Ethics** guidelines to align with ethical standards.

### For Funders and Policymakers
- Read the **Vision & Purpose** and **Sustainability & Scaling** sections.

## 1.2 The THiNK Voice Collection Platform

At the heart of this initiative lies the **Voice Collection Platform**, an open-source, community-driven application for capturing, validating, and sharing voice data. The platform is accessible via the Google Play Store and made freely accessible to contributors.

### Core Features
- **Accessibility:** Optimised for Android devices, with support for offline recording and upload when connectivity is available.  
- **Consent-first design:** Built-in audio and text-based consent scripts in local languages.  
- **Gamified engagement:** Contributors gain recognition through badges, leaderboards, and certificates.  
- **Multilingual interface:** Prompts available in both English and local languages for ease of use.  
- **Data security:** Recordings encrypted and anonymised before storage.  
- **Open standards:** Export options in WAV, JSON, and XML formats for interoperability.  

### Target Languages for Initial Deployment
- **Kikuyu** – spoken in Central Kenya, representing one of the country’s largest ethnic groups.  
- **Dholuo** – widely spoken in Western Kenya, with regional influence across East Africa.  
- **Maasai** – spoken in Kenya and Northern Tanzania, representing a culturally significant community.  
- **Kalenjin** – a Nilotic language cluster in Kenya, particularly important in rural and semi-urban contexts.  
- **Somali** – a Cushitic language spoken across the Horn of Africa, including Kenya, Somalia, and Ethiopia.  

These languages were prioritised due to their sociolinguistic importance, digital underrepresentation, and community readiness for participation in open voice data collection.

## 1.3 Glossary of Key Terms

- **Annotation:** The process of labelling data, often including transcription or translation of recorded speech.  
- **Corpus (plural: Corpora):** A structured set of language data (text or audio) used for linguistic and computational analysis.  
- **Data Sovereignty:** The right of communities to govern and control their data, including how it is collected, stored, and used.  
- **Low-resource language:** A language lacking sufficient digital resources for natural language processing applications.  
- **Metadata:** Descriptive information attached to a dataset (e.g., speaker’s age, gender, or region).  
- **Natural Language Processing (NLP):** A branch of artificial intelligence that enables machines to interpret and generate human language.  
- **Speech Recognition:** The process of converting spoken language into written text.  
- **Text-to-Speech (TTS):** The conversion of written text into spoken voice output.  
- **Validation:** The process of reviewing and confirming the quality and accuracy of data collected.
